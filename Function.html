Overview of the Bypass Circuit

What it does

When leak testing a workpiece with a large internal volume, reaching the specified test pressure takes time. Using a bypass circuit can greatly reduce this charging time. It is particularly effective for low-pressure, large-volume workpieces (50 kPa or less, 3 L or more).

The bypass unit also includes an exhaust valve, which shortens exhaust time and prevents contaminants from the workpiece entering the tester.

Benefits

  • Shorter measurement time — Large-bore valves shorten charging time.
  • Shorter exhaust time — Large-bore valves also shorten exhaust time.
  • Highly reliable bypass valves — Dedicated large-bore, air-operated leak-tester valves provide high reliability and durability.
  • Valve control by the leak tester — The leak tester automatically controls the bypass unit valves using pilot pressure.

Bypass Circuit Schematic

Bypass arrangement with two charging valves — the bypass circuit runs alongside the leak-test circuit.

What Is a Bypass Circuit?

During charging, pressure is supplied through large-diameter piping along a separate route from the leak-test path. This separate route is the bypass circuit.

Two or three charging valves can supply pressure simultaneously to different points on the workpiece, bringing it to the specified pressure more quickly. Increasing the number of bypass routes can reduce the time even further.

Workpieces with Complex Internal Structures

The Problem

Small inlets, multiple internal chambers or restricted flow passages can slow charging, especially when chambers are connected in series. This effect becomes greater at test pressures of 50 kPa or less.

The Solution

Use a bypass unit. Separate, independent charging paths shorten charging time — particularly effective at test pressures of 50 kPa or less.

Pressure Stabilization in the Regulator Section

  • A mechanical precision regulator gives better charging repeatability than an electropneumatic regulator, but response can be sluggish at 50 kPa or less.
  • Use two regulator stages plus an air bleed valve.
  • Set the pressure difference between stages to ~100–150 kPa (e.g. 200 kPa first stage for a 100 kPa test pressure).
  • Lowering the second-stage inlet pressure and maintaining airflow keeps the orifice slightly open, eliminating sluggish response.

Features of the K-Type Through Exhaust Path

Air inside the workpiece is exhausted through Workpiece 1, 2 and 3.

  • Simultaneous exhaust from every port prevents exhaust delays.
  • Prevents delays in transmission of the leak signal if the workpiece leaks.

Summary

Mastering is a function that searches for the most appropriate compensation amount, complying with test conditions or environments, using the actual Work. Mastering allows high-accuracy air leak testing in a shorter time. Also, just having one Master is good enough, as Mastering can be used for various Work.

Merit of Mastering

Shorten Test Time

Test time can be shortened by compensating for temperature effects caused by adiabatic compression.

Less Maintenance Man-hour

Man-hour at maintenance or start-up time can be reduced, as Mastering function is carried out automatically.

Graph Shows Compensation Amount

Mastering operation is displayed on the tester screen as a waveform graph, so you can see whether the correct compensation amount is properly detected or not.

Just One Master Does All!

One master chamber can be used for various Work with different internal volumes. Therefore, just one master chamber is good enough.

The master chamber is small, airtight and maintenance-friendly, compared with using a real Work piece as the Master.

Principle of Mastering

Generally, the temperature of a Work piece changes due to adiabatic compression at the time of pressurization, following a curve like Newton's law of cooling.

Leaks are usually detected in the DET process. However, if a temperature change takes place during DET, correct measurement cannot be carried out, as it heavily affects the leak test. In the past, a Master chamber was used to offset these temperature effects.

Mastering detects the difference between the pressure differences in the actual leak test and that of when the temperature effect ends, after iterating the DET process a preset number of times. That difference becomes the Mastering value (compensation amount), allowing a high-accuracy leak test in a shorter time.

Mastering Operation

In Mastering, the BAL and DET processes are iterated (looped) a preset number of times (N times). The difference between "a" — the pressure difference value in the normal detection process (DET 1) — and "b" — the final value once the temperature effect ends — becomes the Mastering value, regarded as the compensation amount for the next test. "b" is thought to be the unchanged leak value and is not included in the compensation amount.

Effect of Equalization Circuit

In the BAL1 process of Mastering, the pressure of the Work and Master is equalized through AV2. This allows correct measurement within the linearity of the differential pressure sensor, without causing an unnecessarily large differential pressure in the DET process, even if the characteristics of the Work and Master differ. Also, the measurement value of each loop can be displayed in a shifting graph by adding the BAL1 process.

Maximum effect is expected when adding the BAL1 process — stabilizing measurement and shortening test time — not only for large-volume Work but also Work with a certain degree of deformation. Note that the mastering effect may vary with test conditions.

Easy Operation

Mastering is carried out in Mastering Mode with the actual Work. A signal, informing that mastering is in action, can be output by time & date setting. Correct measurement value is obtained even when using an NG Work (a Work with a certain amount of leaks).

Data Shifting Graph

The Mastering value, as a compensation amount for the leak test, is displayed at the time Mastering completes. Whether Mastering is properly carried out or not can be visually checked using the shifting graph.

Limit Setting & Ambient Temperature Adaptation

Limit Setting Avoids Excessive Compensation

With the limit setting function, excessive compensation can be avoided.

Ambient Temperature Adaptation + Auto Drift Compensation

This function searches for the compensation value using the average data of OK products. A stable leak test is allowed, as this can compensate for drifts by adapting to gentle changes in ambient temperature after Mastering.

Important Notes

  • Note 1 — Mastering cannot be carried out when the Work has internal leaks: The leak rate tends to gradually decline and finally end, as well as effects of adiabatic change, if there is an internal leak in the Work. In that case, a detection value after the second and further leak tests will be smaller than the first one. If Mastering is carried out under such circumstances, the leak rate of the first loop is regarded as the Mastering value, which leads to an inappropriate compensation value.
  • Note 2 — Noise cannot be compensated by Mastering: Noise from sealing parts of a jig, tubing/nylon tube, or deformation of the Work cannot be compensated by Mastering. However, compensation might be effective when deformation has declining repeatability. This includes noise generated when Work temperature differs from ambient temperature (e.g. a seal jig), and leaks not originating from the Work — internal leaks generated in a void space of the Work simply stay internal.

Summary

This function sets up an extra limit setting (an "uncertain area") outside the normal limit setting. If test results fall into this uncertain area, the DET process is repeated to ensure a correct judgment, rather than just carrying out exhaust. That allows detecting only the genuine leak element, as repeating the DET process can reduce Noise except for real leaks.

Removal of Noise at DET Time

Noise elements (effects of heat or deformation, etc.) add extra leak rate and could cause a mis-judgment, even when a genuinely leaking piece is judged OK. This symptom especially happens when tact time is short. In general, this Noise can be removed by allowing sufficient time for stabilization.

Automatically Repeat DET Process in the Extra Limit Setting Area

  • In the repeat DET process, the Noise element is removed due to the longer DET time — meaning the 2nd or 3rd DET process can focus only on changes caused by an actual leak.
  • The DET process automatically repeats if a result falls into the extra limit setting area, detecting unstable Work in a stricter way and improving the yield rate of the whole production without prolonging tact time.
  • Mastering will not be available while the DET process is repeating.

Differential Pressure to Leak Rate Management

A leak of gas or fluid is determined by its viscosity, pressure difference, and the size of the leak hole. However, in the actual Differential Pressure method, only air pressure is supplied to the Work and the pressure change is detected as a leak. With this method, the value of pressure difference differs even when the tester indicates the same leak rate, for Work pieces of different internal volume.

Cosmo's air leak tester is equipped with an Automatic Leak Calibration system that converts pressure difference to leak rate (mL/min). This system allows easy quality control management, such as daily sensitivity checks and leak rate calibration.

Equivalent Internal Volume K(Ve)

Equivalent Internal Volume K(Ve) is the constant that converts the amount of differential pressure change, set in the DET process, to leak rate by DET time. The relation between leak rate Q and pressure difference ΔP is: Q = K(Ve) × ΔP ⁄ (1.013×10⁵) × 60 ⁄ T, where Q is the leak rate (mL/min), ΔP is the pressure difference (Pa), T is the DET time (sec), and K(Ve) is the Equivalent Internal Volume (mL).

Two methods are available in the leak tester to calculate Equivalent Internal Volume: the first by flow reference (Leak Master), and the second by generating a pressure difference through volumetric change. Equivalent Internal Volume can also be called the Leak coefficient, indicated as "Ve" in theoretical expressions.

Calculate K(Ve) by Leak Master

  • Calculate Equivalent Internal Volume K(Ve) by applying a given leak rate to the Work measurement system.
  • Select the most appropriate Leak Master from the LM-1B-J1 series, based on the internal volume of the Work and test pressure.
  • Calculate K(Ve) via calibration mode, after installing the Leak Master to the Work CAL port of the tester.

Note: Leak Rate Varies with Barometric Pressure

Even for the same Work, the leak rate tested at a low-barometric-pressure place (such as high altitude) is larger than one tested at a high-barometric-pressure place (such as low altitude). We recommend using a Leak Master to control leak rate in such cases — one calibrated at normal barometric pressure allows no environmental effects when calculating Equivalent Internal Volume.

Leak Coefficient by Volumetric Change

K(Ve) can also be calculated from the pressure difference generated by a volumetric change during the DET process, using K(Ve) = V × P ⁄ ΔP, where V is the volumetric change (mL), P is the test pressure — absolute pressure (Pa), and ΔP is the pressure difference caused by the volumetric change (Pa).

Auto Leak Calibrator (ALC)

Calculate K(Ve) by Volumetric Change

Conversion of pressure difference (Pa) to leak rate (mL/min) in the DET process is allowed by calculating K(Ve). K(Ve) can be calculated from the pressure difference generated by applying a certain amount of volumetric change (ΔV) to the Work measurement system in the DET process: K(Ve) = V × P ⁄ ΔP.

The volumetric change method has two ways: (1) ALC — generates automatic volumetric change, and (2) Manual Leak Calibrator.

Calibration Action by ALC

Volumetric change applying to the Work is adjusted in advance by the ALC dial. Once CAL Mode is activated, K(Ve) is calculated through a process that detects the pressure difference generated by the volumetric change as well as by factors other than volumetric change.

ALC Built-in Intelligent Pneumatic Circuit

The ALC is integrated directly into the tester's pneumatic circuit, alongside the air-operated valves (AV1–AV3), DPS sensor and pressure source, so volumetric change can be generated and measured without any external tooling. Note that the ALC (Auto Leak Calibrator) is not a standard specification.

Difference Between Daily-Check Leak Master & Calibration One

It is better to have a Leak Master with a large leak rate when calculating K(Ve). A Leak Master with a large leak rate allows more accurate calculation of K(Ve), as the ratio of pressure difference (a) to error factors — such as deformation or temperature effects other than the leak itself — gets smaller.
